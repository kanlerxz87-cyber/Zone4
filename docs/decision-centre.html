The order to work in

Four steps, in this order. Skipping to step four is what produces sites that look finished and cannot earn.

Read the suggestions, P1 first

Each carries the problem, the proposal, the cost, the risk and the check that would prove it worked. P3 items are housekeeping and can wait.

Answer the questions each one depends on

A suggestion that needs a Partner Tag cannot be built before the tag exists. The questions page names the owner of each answer, so nothing sits in the wrong queue.

Record the choice in the decision log

Options, cost and reversibility are already written out. Recording a choice is a one-line entry, and the site keeps behaving exactly as it does now until it is recorded.

Only then does anything change

Changes ship through a pull request that must pass the build, the verifier and the QA audit: links, per-row sourcing, disclosure, no prices or ratings, no Amazon marks in titles. A broken build cannot be published.

Choose a path: the open decisions as three coherent orders

Three ways to work through the same queue, each a recommendation assembled from the register at build time - not a recorded choice. Every step names a decision that is open right now (the build refuses to render one that is not), and each row carries the decision's own official source. Pick one path and answer it top to bottom, or ignore all three and work the numeric queue on the Decision log; the queue, not this section, is the source of truth.

What this site does with an answer

A recorded decision becomes a suggestion marked for implementation, then a pull request, then a build that has to pass the same gates as every page here: every table row sourced to an official page, the Associates disclosure on every page, no price or star rating, no Amazon mark in a title or a target keyword, and no unverified value published. The gates are listed on policy compliance and enforced by scripts/verify.py and qa/audit_site.py.

The full audit trail of this project, including the sessions that built it, is in COORDINATION.md in the repository. Every flag published on the site names the source it was checked against; the summaries of what is still missing are on Information gaps.
