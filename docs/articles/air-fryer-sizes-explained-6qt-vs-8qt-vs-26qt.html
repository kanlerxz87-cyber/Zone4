Size is a counter question before it is a capacity question

The size argument in an air fryer listing usually happens in quarts. The size argument at home happens in inches. The three products in this research set list depths of 13.38, 16.9 and 12.75 inches respectively, and those three numbers decide where the machine can physically live - which is why this guide starts with a tape measure, not a capacity figure. Quarts and liters: the labels do not convert for you

Here is a mess the listings themselves make: the dual-basket product's title says 6 Quart while its item details list 6 liters, the French-door oven lists 24.6 liters, and the single-basket fryer lists 8 quarts. This site reports each figure exactly as listed and does not convert between the units - so treat capacity as a rough ordering (the 24.6-liter oven is the big one), not as an arithmetic comparison. The compact pair: what six to eight quarts covers

For one to three people, the choice is between the two smaller formats. The dual-basket unit splits its 6 quarts into two independent 3-quart baskets with a Sync Finish option named in the title - a main and a side aimed at the same finish line. The single-basket fryer spends its 8 quarts on one cavity with four presets and a dishwasher-safe basket. Editorial judgement, from those listed facts: cook two things at once often, take the dual basket; cook one thing big, take the single. M-G2-F1M-G2-F7M-G2-F5M-G2-F9

When the 26-quart oven earns its footprint

The French-door toaster oven lists 24.6 liters of interior and 24 one-touch presets in its title, with a dual-speed fan and rotisserie named among its features. It is the format that can replace a toaster and a countertop oven - and it asks for 16.9 inches of counter depth to do it. If your counter run is shallower than that, this class of oven is out before the cooking questions start. M-G2-F3M-G2-F4M-G2-F8

The counter-depth test, with the arithmetic

Do this before anything else: measure the counter where the machine would sit, front to back. Under about 13 inches of depth, the single-basket unit (12.75 inches listed) is the only one of the three that fits as listed. Around 14 inches, the dual basket (13.38 inches listed) joins it. Past 17 inches, the French-door oven (16.9 inches listed) is finally in play. The room in front of the machine matters too - the listed figures count the unit, not the hands using it. A size checklist before you click

So: measure the counter; decide whether two dishes finishing together is a weekly need (that is the dual basket's whole pitch); decide whether one big basket beats two small ones for how you cook (four presets versus two independent cavities); and read capacity in its own listed unit rather than converting.
