Hi-Fry, Sync Finish, Shake Reminder: what these feature names do and do not tell you

Nine feature names appear across three air fryer listings. Here is each one exactly as the listing states it, which names repeat, and where the definition has to come from.

The feature row is a list of labels, not a specification

Every air fryer listing has a field labelled Special Feature, and it is a comma-separated list of names the seller selected, not measurements. The three listings this site tracks carry nine distinct names between them. The dual-basket machine lists 2Min Button, Automatic Shut-Off, Dual Zone Features, Hi-Fry and Mute. The French-door oven lists Automatic Shut-Off, Programmable, Temperature Control and Timer. The single-basket machine lists Automatic Shut-Off, LED Shake Reminder and Programmable. No row states a duration, a decibel figure, a temperature or a number of zones - the field records that a thing exists on the machine, and that is the whole of what it promises. When a name does come with a number

Hi-Fry is the exception, and it is worth understanding why. The single-basket listing puts the figure in the title itself: '450°F Hi-Fry Option for Crispier Results'. On that machine the name arrives with the number attached, so you know what Hi-Fry means as a setting: the upper end of a range whose rest is stated elsewhere as 200-400°F. The dual-basket listing names Hi-Fry in its title and its feature row but attaches no figure there; its stated range, 200-450°F, appears in its own descriptive text. Same word, two different amounts of support on the page - which is the practical lesson of this whole list. A feature name is worth as much as the number printed next to it. M-G5-F4M-G5-F5M-G5-F6

One name appears on all three listings

Automatic Shut-Off is the only label all three listings claim. Treat that as a category expectation rather than a differentiator: in this price-and-format band the field is where sellers tick the boxes Amazon offers, and a box three out of three sellers tick tells you the feature is table stakes. It does not tell you the three machines behave alike. The cut-off temperature, the timeout and whether the beep can be silenced are not in that row on any of the three pages. M-G5-F8M-G5-F8M-G5-F8

Names that appear once, and the question to ask about each

The singletons are where a listing is genuinely different, and each has one obvious follow-up. Mute on the dual basket: which sounds does it silence, and can it be set back? 2Min Button: a two-minute add, or a two-minute reminder? LED Shake Reminder on the single basket: does it light, sound, or both, and at what point in the cycle? Temperature Control and Timer on the oven, next to a Programmable claim: programmable for how many of its presets? None of those answers is on the page this site reads, so this page does not guess them either. They are in the manual, which the listing links to at most indirectly. M-G5-F1M-G5-F2M-G5-F3

Titles carry more feature names than the details field does

Sync Finish and Easy View Windows - the two names people search for when comparing dual-basket machines - do not appear in the dual basket's Special Feature row at all. They appear in its title. The same pattern holds on the oven, whose title segment names a preset count and an accessory count that its details field leaves out. When you compare two listings, read the title as a second, less controlled feature list: it is where the seller puts the claims they most want found, and it changes more often than the table does. This site has seen one of its own tracked titles change wording twice, which is why every quoted title here carries the date it was read. M-G5-F4M-G5-F7

Where the definition actually lives

The oven listing links to a brand store page, and that is the shape of the answer: named features are defined by the manufacturer's own material, not by the marketplace row that advertises them. Before you pay for a machine because of a feature name, open the brand's manual or support page for that model and check three things - what triggers the feature, whether it can be turned off, and what number, if any, the maker attaches to it. If the maker states none, the feature is a behaviour, not a specification, and it should not outweigh a measured figure like capacity or depth in your decision. M-G5-F9

How to use this list on a shortlist of two

Put the two Special Feature rows side by side and cross out every name both machines claim; what survives is the actual difference, and it is usually one or two items rather than six. Then check each survivor for a number on the page, and treat the ones without a number as questions for the manual. Prices and star ratings are deliberately absent from this site, so the comparison is on listed specifications alone - the live price is always one click away on the listing itself. M-G5-F10
