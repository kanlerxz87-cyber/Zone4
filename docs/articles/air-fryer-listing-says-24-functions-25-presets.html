24 functions or 25 presets? How to read a number a listing gives you twice

One oven lists 24 cooking functions in its title and heads its feature list with 25 presets. Both readings, where each sits on the page, and what to write down on your list.

The case

The French-door oven this site tracks states its function count two ways on one page. Its title has read '24 One-Touch Preset Options' on the two most recent dated readings, and on an earlier reading the same position in the title read '24 cooking functions'. Its 'About this item' section heads the feature list '25 COOKING PRE-SETS'. Amazon states the discrepancy nowhere on the page, and this page will not invent a reconciliation: what a shopper can do is notice that the headline number and the list number are different numbers, in different fields, maintained by the same seller. Count the named modes yourself

Under the 25 heading, the listing names eleven modes: Ribs, Defrost, Bake, Toast, Chicken, Pizza, Pastry, Slow Cook, Roast, Reheat and Keep Warm. Eleven named modes, a heading that says 25 and a title that says 24. The number that should matter to you is the one you can verify by counting on the page, because it is the one that corresponds to buttons you will actually press. If a mode you cook by is not in that list of eleven, neither headline count is going to add it. M-G6-F3

The same page does it again with accessories

The title segment says 'Six Cooking Accessories Included'. The product description on the same page says seven, and names three of them (an air fry basket, a rotisserie spit and a bake pan) with an 'and more'. This site publishes no accessory count for that reason. It is the same class of problem as the preset count, one field apart: a number in a marketing line that the detail on the page does not sustain. M-G6-F4

And a third time with capacity

Capacity shows the pattern most clearly, because the unit changes as well as the number. That oven is named 26QT in its title and records 24.6 liters in its details table. The dual basket is named 6 Quart and records 6 liters - and six US quarts and six litres are not the same volume, so both figures cannot describe the same unit. Neither is a rounding error you can ignore when a recipe or a pan has to fit: they are two claims from one seller, and the table row is the one that sits in the field Amazon validates. Why titles disagree with themselves over time

There is a fourth reason not to trust a headline number blindly: the headline moves. This site read one wording of this listing's title on 2026-11-14, a different one on 2026-11-17 and the second wording again on 2026-11-19, with the ASIN unchanged throughout. A screenshot or a quote you copy into a shortlist is therefore only as good as its date. Every number this site publishes carries the page it was read from and the day it was read, including on this article's own table below. M-G6-F7

What to write on your list

Three rules come out of this. Take measurements from the details table, never from the title, and carry the unit with the figure. Take feature counts from the named list you can count, not from either headline. Date anything you copy, because the seller can change the sentence without changing the product. Applied here: 24.6 liters and 16.9 inches deep are the numbers to plan a counter around, eleven named modes are what the preset list supports, and no accessory count is settled. Prices and ratings are not shown on this site at all, so the listing stays the place where those live. M-G6-F8M-G6-F9
