Air fryer cleanup and counter space: a checklist for the parts a listing leaves out

A checklist for the two things a listing never shows: where the appliance lives and how it gets cleaned. Built from the listed depth, width and dishwasher-safe fields of three air fryers, with every number linked to the page it came from.

Disclosure: As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. This article reports listed specifications; no unit was tested, and the shop links are paid links.

Two questions the listing never answers

Air fryer listings are good at functions and bad at the two things that decide whether you keep using the appliance: whether it fits where you want to put it, and how much of it you have to wash by hand. Neither question needs a lab. Both can be answered from fields the listings already print, plus a tape measure and a look at your own counter. M-G5-F1M-G5-F1M-G5-F1M-G5-F4

Measure the run, not the counter

The three machines here list depths of 13.38, 12.75 and 16.9 inches, and widths of 11.81, 13.5 and 15.8 inches. Depth decides whether the appliance sits under a cabinet or overhangs the front edge of the counter; width decides whether it shares a run with the kettle and the coffee machine. Write both numbers down for the gap you actually have, then check them against the listing. A 24-inch counter is deep enough for all three; the run around them is usually the binding constraint, not the counter itself. M-G5-F1M-G5-F1M-G5-F1M-G5-F2

What is actually dishwasher-safe

This is where marketing language earns its reputation. The dual-basket fryer states that its two nonstick frying baskets are dishwasher-safe. The single-basket fryer states that its basket is. The French-door oven's stored fields carry no dishwasher-safe claim at all. That is not a claim that it cannot go in a dishwasher - it is an absence, and this guide leaves it as an absence rather than filling it in. M-G5-F3M-G5-F4M-G5-F5

Materials and what they mean for wiping

The listings name their materials: metal and plastic for the dual basket, plastic and stainless steel for the single basket, stainless steel for the oven. Materials are a rough signal for how the outside will look after a month of cooking, not a cleaning instruction. The instruction you need is the one in the maker's own guide, which is not part of this site's verified field set. M-G5-F6M-G5-F6M-G5-F6

Four questions to ask before buying a multi-function appliance

First: which parts come out, and which of those does the maker say are dishwasher-safe? Second: how deep and how wide is it, and does that fit the run you have rather than the counter in general? Third: which of its functions would you actually use in a normal week - a preset list is a menu, not a habit. Fourth: what does the maker's documentation say about cleaning the heating element and the interior, and is that somewhere you can reach? None of these four needs a review score to answer. The checklist, one screen

Depth and width written down for the real gap. The listed figures for the machine you are considering, checked against them. The parts the maker names as dishwasher-safe, highlighted on the listing. The functions you would use in a normal week, circled. The maker's cleaning instructions, found and read. If a listing is silent on one of these, treat it as silent - not as a no, and not as a yes.
