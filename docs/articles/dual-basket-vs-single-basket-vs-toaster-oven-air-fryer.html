Dual basket, single basket or toaster oven: comparing three countertop air fryers

Three countertop air-fryer formats compared on listed capacity, footprint, material and features: units kept exactly as listed, blank cells explained, and every figure traced to its official source.

Three formats, one category

All three products sit in the same official category, Home & Kitchen > Kitchen & Dining > Small Appliances > Fryers > Air Fryers, but they answer different cooking problems. The dual-basket oven cooks two dishes at once; the single-basket fryer keeps things simple and compact; the French-door toaster oven is the multi-function, large-capacity option. Choosing between them is a format decision first, and the numbers below only matter once the format fits the kitchen. M-CMP-F1M-CMP-F2M-CMP-F3M-CMP-F6

Capacity, with its unit attached

The tables list the dual-basket oven at 6 liters while its title says 6 quart, the single-basket fryer at 8 quarts, and the French-door oven at 24.6 liters while its title says 26QT. No conversion is offered here on purpose: the listings state different units, and converting them would put this site's arithmetic between the reader and the source. Compare like with like, and carry the unit. M-CMP-F1M-CMP-F2M-CMP-F3

Footprint: the spec that decides fit

Listed footprints are 13.38 by 11.81 by 10.63 inches for the dual-basket oven, 12.75 by 13.5 by 10 inches for the single-basket fryer, and 16.9 by 15.8 by 14.8 inches for the French-door oven. The single-basket unit is the most compact. The dual-basket trades counter space for cooking two dishes at once. The French-door oven needs the most room, and its own page carries a 24-versus-25 function-count discrepancy that its article explains before anyone buys. M-CMP-F1M-CMP-F2M-CMP-F3
