P1 - Decide first

Decisions that gate everything else. 5 suggestions.

S-01P1
Awaiting decision

Put an Associates tag on every outbound product link, or stop calling them affiliate links

The three supplied link.amazon short links resolve to the correct product pages, but the resolved URLs carry no tag= parameter, so a click cannot be credited to an Associates account. Flags F-03 and F-04 record this. The site currently calls these links 'paid links' next to a disclosure that says the site earns from qualifying purchases.

What to change. Sign in to Associates Central, generate a Special Link for each of the three ASINs under the account's own Partner Tag, and replace the three short links site-wide with the tagged links. If no Associates account exists yet, relabel the calls to action as plain product links and withdraw the earning claim until one does.

Why it is worth doing. Every other revenue step depends on this one. Untagged links produce clicks the programme cannot attribute, so the site cannot earn regardless of how good the content is. The disclosure sentence is also only accurate when the links actually carry the tag.

Cost
About 15 minutes once account access exists: three links to build, one data field per product to update, one rebuild.
Risk
Wrong or duplicate tags split attribution. Amazon requires the tag on the link, and the Associates Operating Agreement Section 5 disclosure must stay on the site either way.
How you would know it worked
Re-resolve each published link and confirm a tag= parameter matching the account's Partner Tag appears, then confirm one test click appears in Associates Central reporting.

Decision: D-01Flags: F-03 F-04

  • Operating Agreement, Section 5 (required disclosure)
  • Program Policies, Participation Requirements (Special Links)
S-02P1
Needs a decision first

Apply for Creators API access so prices and ratings can be published lawfully

Prices, star ratings and review counts are deliberately blank across the site because the Associates Participation Requirements allow them only when Amazon serves the link or the data comes from an approved Amazon API (Section 2(b) and 6(t)). Twelve of the site's twenty-one pages therefore tell a reader less than the product page does. Flag F-02.

What to change. Enrol in Amazon Associates, reach the qualifying-sales prerequisite Amazon states for API registration, register for the Creators API through Associates Central, and feed a cached, timestamped price and rating through the build. Until that exists, keep the cells blank exactly as they are now.

Why it is worth doing. A buying guide without a price or a rating is a specification sheet. Readers comparing three air fryers will leave for a page that answers the obvious question, and search engines have little reason to prefer this page over the listing itself. Adding price and rating is the single largest reader-value gain available.

Cost
Prerequisite sales volume is the real cost and it cannot be shortcut; registration itself is an administrative step. The build change is small: one cached field per product plus the existing disclosure.
Risk
Amazon's own documentation states the API is only for the marketplace registered and requires prior written approval for certain reuse. A stale cached price shown as current would be a consumer-protection problem as well as a policy one, so the timestamp must be visible.
How you would know it worked
A published price carries a visible observation timestamp and reconciles with the value the API returned in the same run; any product without a fresh value shows blank, not an old number.

Decision: D-02Flags: F-02 F-16 F-33 F-35

  • Creators API introduction: access prerequisites, including the qualifying-sales requirement
  • Participation Requirements 2(b) prices and availability; 6(t) ratings and reviews
S-03P1
Implemented 2026-11-16

Say plainly who writes the site, and how the content is produced

No page states who is responsible for the content, and the articles read as machine output: identical structure, identical sentence rhythm, the same hedged qualifier in every piece. Google's guidance on people-first content asks who created a page, how it was created, and why, and treats unmanaged generative output as low effort; it also warns that fabricated author identities are a deception signal. The honest answer here is 'a research pipeline, reviewed by the site owner', and that is publishable.

What to change. Add a short editorial note, repeated in the article footer: what the pipeline collects, what a human reviews before publishing, the date of the last review, and a contact route. Do not invent an author name, headshot or credential. If the owner is willing to be named as the reviewer, name them; if not, describe the review process without inventing a person.

Why it is worth doing. This is the difference between a site a reader trusts and one that reads as bulk output, and it is what the 'does not read like AI slop' request is actually about. It also converts an unavoidable fact, automation, into a stated method rather than something a reader has to guess.

Cost
One data field, one template block, one rebuild. Under an hour.
Risk
A vague or boilerplate editorial note is worse than none. It has to name a real review step; if no human reviews the copy, the note must say so.
How you would know it worked
Every article page shows the note; the note names a review step that actually happened on the stated date; no invented person appears anywhere on the site.

S-05P2
Implemented 2026-11-16

Mark the affiliate links rel="sponsored" without breaking the required disclosure

Outbound product links carry rel="noopener noreferrer" only. Google's link-qualification guidance says paid links should be qualified with rel="sponsored" (or nofollow), and its spam policy treats undisclosed payment-for-links arrangements as link spam. Amazon separately requires the visible disclosure, which no attribute can replace.

What to change. Add rel="sponsored" alongside the existing rel values on the three product calls to action, and keep both the adjacent '(paid link)' label and the Operating Agreement sentence exactly where they are.

Why it is worth doing. It costs one attribute and removes a plausible reading of the outbound links as unqualified paid links. It does not change what a reader sees, so it cannot cost conversions.

Cost
One template change, one rebuild.
Risk
None identified for the reader. The temptation to treat the attribute as a substitute for the disclosure must be resisted; the guidance is explicit that qualification does not replace a visible disclosure, and Amazon's rule does not accept an attribute in place of the sentence.
How you would know it worked
Every outbound product link carries rel="sponsored" and still sits next to a paid-link label and the disclosure sentence; the QA audit fails if either the attribute or the disclosure goes missing.

Decision: D-05Flags: —

  • rel="sponsored" for paid links
  • Link spam: paid links must be qualified
  • Associates help: disclosure must be clear and conspicuous, placed near the link

S-12P2
Implemented 2026-11-16

Decide the site's voice, and rewrite the articles to it once

The copy is accurate and unreadable. Every article opens by defining its own category, every paragraph ends with a hedging clause, and the same rhetorical shape repeats five times. Nothing on the site makes a recommendation, which is what a buyer came for. The cause is a writing rule that permits only restatement of verified fields; the effect is that the site answers nothing.

What to change. Keep the fact rule and change the writing rule: allow the writer to conclude, rank within the verified facts, and address the reader directly, while every factual claim still resolves to a sourced row. Rewrite the five pieces to that standard and let the fact table carry the audit trail instead of the prose.

Why it is worth doing. This is the difference between the site as it reads now and a polished marketing site. A comparison page that refuses to compare is a table with sentences around it. Allowing judgement on top of verified facts adds originality without adding risk, and Google's guidance on helpful content rewards exactly that while treating pure restatement as low value.

Cost
Two days for five pieces plus review, most of it writing.
Risk
The moment the writer is allowed to conclude, the fact rule starts to slip. The guard has to be mechanical: a claim without a fact id fails the build, and a ranking claim must cite the field that supports it.
How you would know it worked
Every paragraph still resolves to at least one fact row; each product article states who the product suits; the comparison page ranks the three formats on at least one listed attribute; no sentence asserts a fact that is not in a fact table.

A prompt-ready design brief

For whoever runs the next design pass: a brief that can be pasted into a design prompt as-is. It encodes the constraints above rather than replacing them; edit it freely before use.

Redesign the Zone4 affiliate research site as a polished, trustworthy buying-advice site for countertop air fryers, without changing its verification rules.

Audience: shoppers choosing between a dual-basket air fryer, a single-basket air fryer and a French-door air fryer toaster oven, who want honest, sourced specifications before they click through to a store.

Visual direction: warm kitchen palette (warm amber accent on deep slate and warm off-white surfaces), one clear type scale, card-based layout with soft shadows and rounded corners, generous whitespace, prominent verification badge. No external fonts or frameworks; one stylesheet; system font stack.

Structure: decision-first landing page (who this is for, what to do next, one primary action); buying guide as the top-of-funnel page linking to a comparison and three product reviews; each review with scannable subheadings, a "who this suits" box built only from cited facts, and one clearly labelled call to action after the substance, with the affiliate disclosure adjacent.

Hard constraints: every table row keeps a link to its official source; no prices, no star ratings, no review counts; no Amazon marks in page titles or keywords; the disclosure stays on every page; no product images unless they come from an approved Amazon-served route; the site never claims testing, ranking or trends it cannot evidence.

Trust layer: keep the Flagged for Review, Open questions, Decision log and Verification pages one click from the homepage; a reader should be able to trace any published number to its official source in one click.
