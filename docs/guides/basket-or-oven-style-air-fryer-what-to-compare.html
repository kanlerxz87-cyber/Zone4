Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Three listings, two shapes. Here is what each shape asks of your counter and your cooking, using only what the official listing pages state.

Start with the shape, not the brand

The three listings we track fall into two shapes. Two are basket fryers: P1 is a dual-basket model whose title names 2 independent 3 Qt nonstick baskets [F1], and P3 is a single-basket 8 Qt model [F5]. The third, P2, is an oven-style unit whose title calls it a French door air fryer toaster oven [F3].

The shape decides the first question. A basket fryer asks how much food fits in one basket. An oven-style unit asks how much fits on its racks and whether you want it to replace a toaster oven.

Compare what each listing actually states

Dual-basket models split the capacity. P1's title names two 3 Qt baskets [F1], so the two baskets together are the figure to compare with a single-basket model's total, not the 6 Qt in the title alone. Check whether both baskets run at the same time or one at a time before you rely on the split. The listing states the feature names; it does not give a test result here, so the page is the place to check.

Oven-style units state a larger footprint. P2's recorded dimensions are 16.9 inches deep, 15.8 inches wide and 14.8 inches high [F4], against 12.75 inches deep for P3 [F6]. Measure your counter and the space above it, not only the depth.

A short checklist before you choose

1. Write down the largest meal you cook in a normal week, and whether it needs one tray or two.

2. Measure the footprint including the door or lid swing, then compare it with the recorded dimensions above.

3. Read the feature list on the listing page and count only the features you will use.

4. If a listing gives a count of functions, check it against the title; where the records disagree, we flag it instead of choosing one (see the flagged items for P2).
