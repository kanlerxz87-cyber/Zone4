How to use this page

Four steps. Step one takes ten minutes: 26 proposals, each with the problem it solves and the check that proves it worked.

  1. Read every proposal. Each has its problem, its proposed change, its cost, its risk and the check that proves it worked. Five carry a dated application from 2026-11-19 - one complete, four partial, each naming exactly what shipped and what still waits on you; the rest are proposals only.
  2. Answer every question, or let the defaults stand. Each question records what is already known, what is missing, who can supply it and how to discover it. Where a default exists it is named in the discovery step, so silence is still a recorded decision rather than a gap.
  3. Mark each proposal Accepted, Rejected or Parked. Record the choice with the date in this register (data/redesign.json). Accepted proposals become the next build's scope; the check field becomes its acceptance test.
  4. Only then build. The build order is the priority order: P1 first, because the tracking-ID test (RQ-02) decides whether any of the rest can earn.

This page is the review gate the brief asks for: a single place where every proposed change, its cost, its risk and the proof it worked sit beside the questions that must be answered before the change is worth making. Four of them have a dated partial or full application from 2026-11-19 (RD-01 fully; RD-03, RD-06 and RD-08 partly), each labelled with what shipped and what still waits on an answer; nothing else is applied. These proposals are filed in their own register and are not duplicated into data/suggestions.json, so no count on any other page moves. Every source link is an official page; where a fact could not be read from an official source it is marked as missing rather than filled in. A second batch (RD-15 to RD-26, filed 2026-11-20 by arena/97ff545d-zone4) feeds the site-design prompt directly — a paste-ready design-prompt v2, question-shaped trendy topic hypotheses under a dated validation rule, cluster architecture, an anti-slop pre-publish gate, a freshness programme, measurement-before-publishing restated as a gate, structured-data hygiene, a disclosed social distribution pack, reader capture, decision receipts, a humanised storefront and an adjacent-niche scorecard — with twelve matching questions (RQ-14 to RQ-25) naming what is known, what is missing, who can supply it and the discovery step. None of these rows is applied.

RD-01P1
Applied 2026-11-19

Give the front page a shopper's headline, not a method statement

The hero currently reads “Find the air fryer that fits your counter — with every number fact-checked.” The promise is about the site's checking, not about the reader's dinner. The design register already filed this pattern as anti-slop finding AS-08 (“governance apparatus in the shop window”), and AS-01 records the same habit in article headlines.

What we would change. Replace the hero H1 and lead with a benefit-first headline and one sentence a shopper would repeat to a friend. Keep a two-line trust row underneath (receipts on every figure / gaps flagged, never hidden) rather than inside the headline. Proposed H1: “Crispy dinners, one counter square.” Proposed lead: “Three air fryers — dual basket, single basket, French-door oven — compared on the numbers their listings state, so you can pick your format in a minute and be sure before you buy.”

What shipped on 2026-11-19. The landing-page H1 and lead now lead with the reader's outcome; the receipts moved to the trust row. Article-level headline questions stay with decision D-37.

Why it is worth doing. A buyer arriving from search or a link decides in about five seconds whether the page is for them. A headline about the checking method answers a question nobody asked yet; the receipts are the proof, not the pitch.

Cost
One string pair in scripts/build_site.py plus a rebuild.
Risk
None to any published product value. The compliance strip and the receipts stay exactly where the gates check them.
How you would know it worked
The H1 names a reader outcome; the word “fact-checked” no longer appears in the H1; the disclosure strip still renders above the main content on all pages; scripts/verify.py and qa/audit_site.py stay green.

RD-03P1
Partly applied 2026-11-19

Show the product, not a grey box: a photography gate with a clean fallback

No product image appears anywhere on the site. Suggestion S-18 and flag F-43 record why: Amazon images are Program Content and may not be copied or hosted here, and generated or stock photography would misrepresent the product. The result is the one thing a shop cannot be — wordless about what the thing looks like.

What we would change. Build three independent image slots that each fall back to a labelled placeholder: (1) owner-supplied original photography with a written release on file; (2) listing images rendered live by the Amazon link/API on each visitor's click — never downloaded, never cached; (3) an illustrative, clearly-labelled diagram of the listed dimensions drawn from the dimensions already sourced in this repository. Ship the diagram now, because it is the only route that needs no new permission; switch a slot to photography or live listing images when the owner supplies the release or the API access.

What shipped on 2026-11-19. The diagram route shipped: scaled footprint diagrams drawn from the listed dimensions appear on the four category tiles and at the top of each category page (docs/imagery.html records every asset). Photographs still need either an owner release or written word from Amazon (RQ-05).

Why it is worth doing. The visual deficit is the largest remaining “looks generated” signal. A dimension diagram is honest, original, and safe, and it answers the counter-space question better than a glossy photo would.

Cost
Half a day for the diagram generator; minutes to flip a slot on when a release or API access exists.
Risk
High if any route is shortcuts. Copying listing images breaches the Operating Agreement's Program Content terms (S-18, F-43). Generated imagery of a real product would also breach the accuracy rules.
How you would know it worked
No image file in the repository was downloaded from a listing; every placeholder is labelled; the dimension diagram is drawn only from sourced values and is captioned with its source; the build adds the image to the sitemap for image search.

RD-14P1
Proposed

Publish the one page a shopper is actually searching for: a fit-verdict hub with the chooser above the fold

The site's pages are strong on method and thin on the page shape a first-time buyer arrives for — “which air fryer fits my kitchen and my cooking”. The comparison page compares and explicitly declines to conclude (anti-slop finding AS-05), the buying guide teaches a five-step method, and the chooser sits below two sections of cards on the landing page. A reader who wants an answer gets a procedure.

What we would change. Build one hub at /air-fryers-that-fit: the three-question chooser first, then a verdict table with one row per reader situation (two dishes at once; one big dish and a shallow counter; hosting on a deep counter), each row naming the product, the listed fields that justify it, the cash-and-counter cost of the alternative, and a decision-labelled link. Above it, a 40-word answer a reader could quote; below it, the method that produced it. It publishes no price, rating or test result — only listed fields already in the repository — so it needs no new access, only the owner's yes on ranking within verified facts (RQ-11).

Why it is worth doing. This is the page that earns the click it is optimised for, and it is the page competitors cannot copy without doing the same sourcing. It also gives every existing article a natural internal target.

Cost
One page assembled from existing fact rows plus the chooser component already built; half a day including the check.
Risk
Medium. A verdict is a claim, so every line must resolve to a listed field (D-12, D-19). If RQ-11 is answered no, the page can still ship as a situation table with no verdict language.
How you would know it worked
Every verdict cell resolves to a fact row with a source link; no price, rating, review count or trend claim appears; the chooser is the first interactive element; the page is in the sitemap, the feed and every article's 'Where next' block.

RD-05P2
Proposed

One weekly editorial item, published on a named day, with the topic chosen from measured questions

The current cadence is one guide every 14 days, and the next topic is chosen by editorial instinct because no search data exists yet. A publication that is never seen updating does not earn a returning reader, and a topic queue built on instinct cannot improve.

What we would change. Publish one item every Tuesday for eight consecutive weeks: four from the existing queue, four seasonal or question-led pieces written from the same fact rows. Record the topic-selection reason for each one in data/publishing.json. Once Search Console is verified (SG-29), the reason field must cite the query that earned the next topic.

Why it is worth doing. A predictable day is how readers and crawlers learn that the site is alive. The reason field is what turns the calendar from a wish list into a measured loop.

Cost
One item per week from existing fact rows; the calendar change is a data edit.
Risk
Cadence commitments that are missed read worse than no cadence. Each item must still carry its fact table and sources; a thin item should slip a week rather than publish.
How you would know it worked
data/publishing.json names the day and the eight dates; each published item has a fact table and appears in sitemap.xml and feed.xml; each has a topic-selection reason.

RD-07P2
Proposed

Publish trustworthy structured data for the store pages

Only WebSite and Article JSON-LD exist. The product pages carry no ItemList or Product structured data, so a search result cannot show the store as a compared set of listings — and the site has no way to earn the richer result.

What we would change. Add ItemList structured data to the shop page and the category pages, listing each product by name, ASIN and listing URL exactly as published.
