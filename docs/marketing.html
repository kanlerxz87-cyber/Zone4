Nothing here needs a review unit or a taste test - only a tape measure and two minutes with the listing.

Capacity is the headline number and the easiest one to misread, because listings mix units freely. The dual-basket unit in this set is titled as a 6-quart machine made of two independent 3-quart baskets, while its details table shows 6 liters; the single basket lists 8 quarts; the French-door oven lists 24.6 liters in its details while its title names a 26QT oven. Carry the unit along with the number, and compare like with like before you compare anything else. M-G4-F1M-G4-F2M-G4-F3

Depth is the number that decides where the machine can physically live. The three products on this site list depths of 13.38, 16.9 and 12.75 inches respectively - a spread of more than four inches between the shallowest and the deepest. Measure the counter you actually have, including the plug and the wall clearance, and read the listed depth as a hard floor. M-G4-F4M-G4-F4M-G4-F4

Width and height decide the rest of the fit: whether two machines can sit side by side, and whether the unit slides under a cabinet. The listings show 11.81 by 10.63 inches for the dual basket, 15.8 by 14.8 inches for the French-door oven, and 13.5 by 10 inches for the single basket. Check height against the underside of your cabinets with the machine's own listed figure, not a photo. M-G4-F5M-G4-F5M-G4-F5

How the cooking space is divided shapes how a weeknight actually runs. The dual basket splits into two independent 3-quart baskets; the single basket is one 8-quart space; the French-door oven is one tall cavity with front doors. Two baskets mean a main and a side finishing together; one cavity means a roast or a pizza. Neither is better - the right answer is the shape of the meals you cook most. M-G4-F6M-G4-F6M-G4-F6

The stated heat range tells you what is possible: the dual basket lists 200 to 450 degrees, and the single basket lists 200 to 400 degrees with a 450-degree Hi-Fry option. Function counts are shakier ground - one listing here says 24 cooking functions in its title and 25 cooking pre-sets in its feature section, and this site reports both counts rather than picking one. Count the functions you will actually use; the tally on the box is not the point. M-G4-F7M-G4-F7M-G4-F8M-G4-F8

Cleanup is the friction you live with daily, and listings describe it in one clause worth reading twice. The dual basket and the single basket both list nonstick, dishwasher-safe baskets; the oven's title names six included accessories while its own page description says seven, so quote neither count as settled. If the parts that touch food cannot go in your dishwasher, the machine will cost you minutes every evening. M-G4-F9M-G4-F9M-G4-F9

Two numbers are deliberately missing from this checklist: the price and the star rating. The programme rules this site operates under keep both off pages like this unless an approved Amazon API supplies them, so the listings show them and this site does not. What you get instead is the arithmetic above, every figure linked to the page it came from - and a set of blanks that say so instead of hiding. M-G4-F10
